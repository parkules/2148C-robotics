03 / Engineering

Our process is
the product.

We document ideas, designs, experiments, problems, testing, and improvements so the development of our robot can be understood and reproduced.

Problem

Identify what needs to work better.

Ideas

Share possible solutions and build from each other's suggestions.

Design → Build

Use CAD, sketches, and construction to turn ideas into a robot mechanism.

Test → Data

Measure performance and record what actually happened.

Analysis → Improvement

Use what we learned to decide what to change next.

04 / Season goals

What we're
working toward.

Our notebook defines goals for the robot, competitions, team, and documentation.

🤖

Robot

Build a reliable robot capable of handling the major tasks in Override, improve it through repeated testing and iteration, and keep it legal while maximizing performance.

🏁

Competition

Prepare for different match situations, develop reliable autonomous routines, improve driving skills, and learn from every competition.

👥

Team

Make sure every member contributes, improve communication, and learn new engineering and programming skills throughout the season.

📓

Notebook

Record major design decisions, successful and unsuccessful designs, testing data, and enough reasoning to make our engineering process understandable.

📈

Data-driven decisions

Use measurements and testing instead of relying only on guesses when deciding what to change.

🧪

Keep testing

Document failures, investigate causes, and connect each iteration to what we learned from the previous one.

06 / Engineering notebook

We document
how we think.

Our notebook is more than a collection of finished designs. It records successes, failures, changes, measurements, and lessons throughout the season.

Problem → Ideas → Design → Build → Test → Data → Analysis → Improvement

Record work as it happens

Important ideas, decisions, tests, measurements, and changes are documented rather than reconstructed from memory.

Explain the reasoning

We record the problem, options considered, and why a particular solution was selected.

Make it reproducible

We include enough information that another team member can understand what we did and, when practical, repeat the test.

07 / Greenville Middle School

School robotics,
teamwork & time.

Because the robot stays at school, the team makes the most of class and after-school practice time through organization, communication, and time management.

Class schedule

The team meets two or three times each week depending on the block schedule. Each class period is approximately 81 minutes.

After-school practice

Additional practices take place Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday to give the team more time to build, test, program, and improve.

Everyone contributes

Builders, programmers, drivers, CAD, and notebook members communicate so separate tasks fit together as one robot.
